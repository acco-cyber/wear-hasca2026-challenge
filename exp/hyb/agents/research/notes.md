## 1. Recording protocol (WEAR paper + supplementary + the challenge's participant_meta_data.txt)
- Paper: "Participants were suggested to follow a two-session setup, i.e. 9 activities per session", but "it was allowed to differ ... split the 18 activities across as many (or as few) sessions as participants liked". Also "roughly 90 seconds" per activity, with breaks allowed. https://arxiv.org/html/2304.05088
- Order: the project page says each participant "had the freedom to choose the order of activities and take breaks as desired" (http://mariusbock.github.io/wear/). Signal Sleuths likewise say "freedom to determine the exact activity sequence" (https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03947v1). **Officially there is no fixed order.**
- The recording plan is in the supplementary PDF, Section D, pp. 22-26 (https://github.com/mariusbock/wear/blob/main/supplementary_material.pdf). I decoded the glyphs:
  - Each session page begins "Each activity 3 sets - repetitions at will (ca. 30 sec); short break after each set (ca. 30 sec)". So one activity is about 3x30 s of work plus 2x30 s of breaks.
  - Plan table order, B2 page: Sidesteps, Butt-kicks, Shoulders, Hamstrings, Lumbar rotation, Burpees, Walking lunges (normal/complex), Bench dips.
  - Plan table order, B1 page: Jogging, Jogging w/ rotating arms (left arm, then right arm, then both), Skipping, Triceps, Lunging, Push-ups (normal/complex), Sit-ups (normal/complex).
  - Your B1/B2 blocks are exactly the plan's two session tables. **Check in train how often the within-block order follows this table order.** If it usually does, that is a strong order prior for HMM/Viterbi decoding.
  - Sync protocol at the start AND end of every session (data_creation README): stand still 10 s, 3 jumps with raised arms, stand still 10 s. That gives distinctive NULL anchors at the chain ends, if they were not cropped (check train). https://github.com/mariusbock/wear/tree/main/data_creation
  - Annotation: a single expert annotated .srt subtitles, and gaps between subtitles are filled with NULL. So the breaks between sets are NULL unless the annotator bridged them (check train).
  - Head camera tilted about 45 deg down, hands/feet centrally visible. Original video is 1080p60; challenge features are VideoMAEv2-Base at 30 FPS.
- Train sessions (supplementary Table 2): most subjects have 2 sessions of 9 activities, often on different days and sometimes at different locations (e.g. sbj_2 and sbj_6). There are odd splits: sbj_0 7/6/7; sbj_3 10/6/2/2; sbj_6 10/8; sbj_2 9/9/1, where session 3 is a single re-done activity, 01:52 long, on another day at another location. sbj_18/19 are repeats of sbj_0/sbj_14 in summer (the challenge files *_2.csv).
- Test cameras (paper): the 4 new test participants (paper era) were filmed with a GoPro Hero 11 instead of the Hero 8, and "two new participants were recorded at a previously unseen location". Those are paper-era test subjects that are now in our train set. For the challenge's sbj_22-25, the hardware is not stated.
- **Challenge test meta (local file data/participant_meta_data.txt; the same 3.7 kB file shipped with the 2025 challenge):**
  - sbj_22: S1 40:30 with 9 acts, S2 40:10 with 8 acts (both early Feb, location 12, city park), **S3 02:57 with 1 act, late Feb, location 13 (different place)**.
  - sbj_23: S1 24:00 with 9 acts, S2 26:21 with 8 acts (late March, location 14), **S3 01:47 with 1 act, "early-February", location 14, sunny (other sessions cloudy)**.
  - sbj_24: 17:25 + 15:50, 9+9 acts, same afternoon, location 15 (= train location 1 with a new concrete walkway).
  - sbj_25: 16:05 + 15:49, 9+9 acts, same midday, location 3 (a train location).
  - My counts from test_meta_data.csv: sbj_22 5197 windows, sbj_23 3128, sbj_24 1995, sbj_25 1914. The session sums in seconds are 5017, 3128, 1995 and 1914. **So sbj_23/24/25 are exactly one window per second of the concatenated sessions (non-overlapping, full coverage).** sbj_22 has 180 more windows than listed; S3 may really be 5:57, or the durations are wrong.
  - Limbs are roughly uniform per subject (e.g. sbj_22: 1285 RL / 1275 RA / 1326 LA / 1311 LL).
- Implications: every subject performs each of the 18 activities exactly once, so each class is one contiguous bout per subject. sbj_22's sessions are about 40 min per 9 activities, versus about 16-25 min in train. **sbj_22 is therefore likely more than 50% NULL**, while sbj_24/25 are roughly 15-25% NULL. The 1-activity S3 of sbj_22 is filmed at a different location, so it should form its own cluster in video space: about 177 windows of one class plus NULL. That class should be the block-mate missing from S2 (8 acts).
- Video/window geometry (Kaggle data page): feature i uses frames i-8..i+7 at 30 FPS. The 15 kept vectors must be frames 8..22 of the window, the only ones whose 16-frame clip stays inside the 30 frames. Consecutive windows therefore give adjacent, non-overlapping clips: the gap from the last vector of window k to the first vector of window k+1 is 16 frames, similar to the 14-frame span inside one window. **Extrapolating the within-window embedding trajectory should predict the successor's first vector.** This is the strongest ordering cue. (https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/3rd-wear-dataset-challenge-hasca-2026/data)
- The video-feature bug fix (discussion /694742) says "Sample ordering is unchanged". So ids are a fixed shuffle and carry no temporal information.

## 2. Previous WEAR challenges
- 2024 (1st challenge, not on Kaggle): the test set was 6 subjects, "untrimmed format, without any segmentation" (continuous, NOT shuffled). The metric was sample-wise macro F1.
  - Winners, Signal Sleuths (Van Der Donckt et al.): 14 features over multi-resolution windows {1,2,4,8,16,32} s, 0.5 s stride, left-right swapping and upper-lower limb pairing, plus a Gaussian smoother (10+10 steps, sigma 6).
  - Their CV: raw 90.01, LR-swap 91.30, limb-pairing 91.87. Smoothing added only +0.3 to 0.9 pp, because the 32 s feature windows already carried the context.
  - https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03947, example report DOI 10.1145/3675094.3678453.
- WEAR paper supplementary Table 5, shallow DeepConvLSTM, 1 s windows, LOSO: **F1 70.36 with no filter vs 75.44 with a 10 s majority-vote filter (+5.1 pp)**. For 0.5 s windows: 65.96 to 72.26 (+6.3 pp). This is the best public estimate of what per-second temporal context is worth for a single-window classifier.
- 2025 (2nd challenge, Kaggle "2nd-wear-dataset-challenge"): **the same 4 test subjects, sbj_id 22-25**, with "random, sensor-specific 1-second sliding windows" plus random augmentations: axis inversion, 180 deg rotation about x, jitter, scaling 0.8-1.2, +/-15 deg rotation, 20% channel masking.
  - Final private LB: Whatever (FAME ensemble, Calatrava-Nicolas et al., DOI 10.1145/3714394.3756194) 0.60249, Schw1Eg0 0.58750, HARMA 0.57773, Signal Sleuths 0.57657. Baselines: A&D 0.47989, DeepConvLSTM 0.44986.
  - **No 2025 report used ordering or temporal reconstruction.** Everyone classified windows independently.
  - Sources: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/2nd-wear-dataset-challenge/leaderboard ; https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23173 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21282
- Open question worth 5 minutes: is the 2025 test.csv the same windows as 2026 (same ids/limbs), or different limb draws or overlapping "sliding" windows of the same seconds? If 2025 used a stride smaller than 1 s, a 2025 window overlapping two 2026 windows (same limb, allowing for augmentation) would directly reveal adjacency. It needs the user to accept the 2025 rules and download. That is external unlabeled data of the test subjects: grey-zone, not banned in the 2026 rules, but it deserves disclosure.

## 4. Literature for re-ordering shuffled clips from embeddings
- **ATSP / Hamiltonian path on a learned asymmetric successor score, solved with LKH-3/Concorde.** Proven on shredded-document strips: Paixao et al. CVPR 2020, self-supervised asymmetric metric plus ATSP, 94.8% accuracy on Western text (https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Paixao_Faster_Reconstruction_of_Shredded_Text_Documents_via_Self-Supervised_Deep_Asymmetric_CVPR_2020_paper.html). For video: Epstein et al. 2021, TSP over frames with edge weight -log p(transition) plus a zero-cost dummy node for open paths (https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02337).
- **"Best buddies" (mutual best successor/predecessor) greedy chaining** for high-precision partial chains (Pomeranz et al. CVPR 2011, https://www.cs.bgu.ac.il/~ben-shahar/Publications/2011-Pomeranz_Shemesh_and_Ben_Shahar-A_Fully_Automated_Greedy_Square_Jigsaw_Puzzle_Solver.pdf). Partial chains alone are enough for smoothing.
- **Spectral seriation (Fiedler vector of the similarity Laplacian)**, Atkins, Boman & Hendrickson, SIAM J. Comput. 1998. It gives a coarse global order and is fragile with repeated or looping states (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.5370 covers the method). Diffusion-pseudotime/MST ordering from single-cell work is the same idea.
- **Gumbel-Sinkhorn (Mena et al. 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08665)** is differentiable permutation learning for jigsaw/sorting. It is overkill here, but Sinkhorn balancing is useful for class marginals (see idea 3).
- Self-supervised order verification to train the successor scorer: Shuffle&Learn (arXiv 1603.08561), OPN sorting sequences (1708.01246), VCOP (Xu CVPR 2019). Photo-sequencing (Basha et al., https://www.eng.tau.ac.il/~avidan/papers/PhotoSeqICCV13.pdf) orders unsynchronised stills.

## Exploitable ideas ranked by expected gain
1. **Timeline reconstruction per subject, then sequence smoothing/decoding.** Train a successor scorer on train windows simulated exactly like test: 15 frames at positions 8..22 of each 30-frame second, random limb.
   - Features: (a) VideoMAE continuity, i.e. last vectors of k versus first vectors of k+1 plus an extrapolated trajectory; (b) IMU boundary continuity when both windows share a limb, with a limb-pair-aware head; (c) label-probability agreement.
   - Build mutual-best chains, or solve ATSP per subject (N up to 5.2k, easy for LKH). Then run Viterbi/HMM or a 10-30 s smoother over class posteriors.
   - Ordering also gives cross-limb fusion: neighbours contribute the other limbs. That is exactly what separates push-ups vs complex, sit-ups vs complex, lunges vs complex and the jogging variants.
   - The paper's 1 s-window evidence is +5 pp F1 from a 10 s majority filter. Even partial chains should capture most of it. **Expected +1.5 to 4 pp. This is very likely what 0.933-0.937 teams do.**
2. **Cheap proxy for idea 1: a per-subject kNN graph in video space with label propagation, or averaging logits over mutual kNN.** No explicit order is needed and it can be validated on train LOSO in hours. Expected +0.5 to 2 pp, and it is the first step toward idea 1.
3. **Per-subject class-marginal constraints.** Each subject performs each of the 18 activities exactly once, for about 90-150 s each; NULL is the remainder (sbj_22 about 50%+, sbj_24/25 about 20%). Run Sinkhorn/OT balancing of posteriors per subject toward those counts, with NULL absorbing the slack and per-class counts taken from train quantiles. This fixes normal-vs-complex over- or under-prediction. Expected +0.5 to 1.5 pp; stacks with ideas 1 and 2.
4. **Session and block structure.** Cluster each subject's windows into sessions: sbj_22 S3 is at another location; sbj_23 S3 is on another date with different weather. Within a 9-activity session only one block (B1 or B2) plus NULL is possible, so mask the other block's 9 classes. The single-activity S3 must be the block-mate missing from S2. Expected +0.3 to 1 pp, mostly from cross-block confusions (lunging-stretch vs lunges, triceps vs shoulders, jogging variants).
5. **Within-block plan order as a prior in the HMM**, only if train confirms that most participants follow the table order. Also use the session start/end sync-jump NULL anchors. Small, but it fixes chain direction and ends.
6. Verify whether the 2025 test.csv (same subjects) adds overlapping or extra-limb windows that act as an adjacency oracle. It is potentially large but uncertain and rules-grey; the user must download it.
